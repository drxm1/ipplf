* Purpose and assumptions

This document derives the continuum and aligned-grid references and the
periodic tilted-plane construction used to interpret plane-pulse reflection and
transmission. The continuum solution is the physical
reference. The discrete solution checks whether an aligned numerical interface
reproduces the recurrence that the scheme is intended to solve. Agreement with
the discrete solution does not establish agreement with the continuum solution.
The discrete reference below is for the Standard stencil with the staggered
O1-VS material treatment; another stencil or constitutive rule needs its own
discrete derivation.

The media considered here are linear, isotropic, lossless and nondispersive,
with relative permeability one and positive relative permittivities
\(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\). There is no prescribed surface charge or
surface current. Formulae use solver units \(c=\varepsilon_0=\mu_0=1\), so
\(n_j=\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}\) and \(\mathbf B=\mathbf H\). These are the
nonmagnetic plane-interface assumptions of [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §§5.6–5.7]].
Dimensional output must be converted consistently before these expressions are
evaluated; amplitude ratios and the power fractions below are dimensionless.

The main derivations assume propagating incident and transmitted waves. Complex
roots are available in the reference code, but an evanescent-wave experiment
also needs a suitable measurement and normalization. It is not established by
the propagating-wave extraction described here.

This is a reference specification, not a set of measured results. Each numerical
comparison must retain its actual inputs, calculated reference values and run
provenance as described in [[provenance][Reference data and reproducibility]].

* Coordinates, amplitudes and Fourier convention

The incidence plane is x-z; y is out of plane. In a local interface coordinate
system, let \(u\) increase from the incident medium 1 into medium 2, with the
face at \(u=0\). Let \(\hat{\mathbf n}\) point along increasing \(u\), keep
\(\hat{\mathbf s}=\hat{\mathbf y}\) fixed, and choose
\(\hat{\mathbf t}=\hat{\mathbf n}\times\hat{\mathbf s}\). The tangent
coordinate is \(v\). For incidence towards +x these directions are
\(\hat{\mathbf n}=\hat{\mathbf x}\) and
\(\hat{\mathbf t}=\hat{\mathbf z}\). The same out-of-plane basis is used
for incident, reflected and transmitted amplitudes. This is a relabelling of
the normal/tangent construction in [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §§5.6–5.7]].

A positive-frequency mode has the convention
\[
 F(\mathbf x,t)=\operatorname{Re}\left\{
 F_0 e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x-i\omega t}\right\},\qquad \omega>0.
\]
The time transform uses the positive sign,
\[
 \widehat F(\omega)=\int F(t)e^{+i\omega t}\,dt.
\]
The code uses forward normalization one. This differs by a constant factor
from the convention printed in [[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, Eq. (4.6)]],
which cancels in consistently normalized ratios. Ellingson uses
\(e^{+i\omega t}\) phasors; when a coefficient is complex, its time-convention
conversion must be made explicitly. Real propagating Fresnel coefficients are
unchanged by that conjugation.

TE amplitudes are ratios of the fixed Cartesian component \(E_y\). TM
amplitudes are ratios of the fixed component \(H_y=B_y\). Write their common
scalar notation as \(F\), with incident amplitude one at the face:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_1(u,v)&=\left(e^{ik_1u}+r e^{-ik_1u}\right)e^{i\kappa v},
       &&u<0,\\
 F_2(u,v)&=t e^{ik_2u}e^{i\kappa v},&&u>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\kappa\) is the conserved signed tangential wavenumber, and
\(k_1,k_2>0\) are normal wavenumber magnitudes for propagating modes. The
coefficient phase is referred to the face. The common tangential phase follows
from matching fields at every point of the planar boundary
[[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, Eqs. (5.122), (5.145)–(5.147) and §5.8]].

* Continuum reflection and transmission

** Normal wavenumbers and incidence angle

The homogeneous dispersion relation and tangential phase matching give
\[
 k_j=\sqrt{\varepsilon_j\omega^2-\kappa^2},\qquad
 \sin\theta_j=\frac{\lvert\kappa\rvert}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}\,\omega}.
\]
The incidence angle \(\theta_1\) is measured relative to the face normal,
not relative to a mesh axis. These are the nonmagnetic form of Snell's law
and the plane-wave dispersion relation in [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §§5.6–5.8]].
For the propagating comparisons below, require
\(\varepsilon_j\omega^2>\kappa^2\) in both media. At a cutoff the normal
flux vanishes and the ordinary incident-flux normalization becomes degenerate.

** TE: fixed electric component

For the selected phasor sign, Faraday's law gives
\(\mathbf H=\mathbf k\times\mathbf E/\omega\). With
\(\mathbf E=E_y\hat{\mathbf s}\), the tangential magnetic component of a
mode with signed normal wavenumber \(k_u\) is \(k_u E_y/\omega\).
Continuity of tangential electric and magnetic fields therefore gives
\[
 1+r_E=t_E,\qquad k_1(1-r_E)=k_2t_E.
\]
Eliminating \(t_E\) yields
\[
 r_E=\frac{k_1-k_2}{k_1+k_2},\qquad
 t_E=\frac{2k_1}{k_1+k_2}=1+r_E.
\]
This derives the wavenumber form of [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, Eqs. (5.142) and
(5.147)]] after replacing impedance and angle by
\(\eta_j=1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}\) and
\(\cos\theta_j=k_j/(\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}\omega)\).

** TM: fixed magnetic component

Ampere's law gives
\(\mathbf E=-\mathbf k\times\mathbf H/(\omega\varepsilon_j)\).
For \(\mathbf H=H_y\hat{\mathbf s}\), the tangential electric component
is \(-k_uH_y/(\omega\varepsilon_j)\). The two boundary equations become
\[
 1+r_H=t_H,\qquad
 \frac{k_1}{\varepsilon_1}(1-r_H)
   =\frac{k_2}{\varepsilon_2}t_H.
\]
Solving them gives
\[
 r_H=\frac{\varepsilon_2k_1-\varepsilon_1k_2}
           {\varepsilon_2k_1+\varepsilon_1k_2},\qquad
 t_H=\frac{2\varepsilon_2k_1}
           {\varepsilon_2k_1+\varepsilon_1k_2}=1+r_H.
\]
These are magnetic-amplitude coefficients. Ellingson's reflected TM basis
uses the opposite magnetic direction, so his electric-field coefficient
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{TM}}\) obeys
\(r_H=-\Gamma_{\mathrm{TM}}\) and
\(t_H=1-\Gamma_{\mathrm{TM}}\). The sign change is explained explicitly
in [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §5.7, Eq. (5.156), Eq. (5.187), and the discussion
preceding Eq. (5.191)]]. A plot of \(r_H\) must not be labelled as that
electric-field reflection coefficient.

** Normal incidence and the Brewster case

At normal incidence, \(\kappa=0\) and \(k_j=n_j\omega\). Substitution in
the preceding coefficients gives
\[
 r_E=\frac{n_1-n_2}{n_1+n_2},\quad
 r_H=\frac{n_2-n_1}{n_1+n_2},\quad
 t_E=\frac{2n_1}{n_1+n_2},\quad
 t_H=\frac{2n_2}{n_1+n_2}.
\]
The opposite signs of \(r_E\) and \(r_H\) describe the chosen component
bases, not different reflected powers. This is the normal-incidence limit
discussed in [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §§5.6–5.7]]. For identical media all
four expressions reduce to zero reflection and unit transmission.

For unequal nonmagnetic media, the TM numerator vanishes when
\(\varepsilon_2k_1=\varepsilon_1k_2\). Squaring and substituting the
dispersion relations gives
\[
 \kappa_B^2=\frac{\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2}
                  {\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2}\omega^2,
 \qquad
 \tan\theta_B=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_2}{\varepsilon_1}}.
\]
This is a derivation of [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, Eq. (5.253)]]. At that angle
\(r_H=0\) and \(t_H=1\). The reflection phase and a relative reflection
error are undefined at an exact zero. Use an absolute complex or magnitude
error there. If the media are identical, reflection vanishes at every angle;
there is no unique Brewster angle to obtain by cancelling their difference.

** Power fractions

Using the time-average Poynting vector
\(\langle\mathbf S\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}
(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H^*)\)
[[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, Eq. (3.98)]], the incoming normal flux of a
propagating mode is
\[
 S_{u,E}=\frac{k_j}{2\omega}\lvert E_y\rvert^2,
 \qquad
 S_{u,H}=\frac{k_j}{2\omega\varepsilon_j}\lvert H_y\rvert^2.
\]
These follow by inserting the field relations used above. Dividing reflected
and transmitted normal fluxes by the incident flux then gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_E&=\lvert r_E\rvert^2,&
 T_E&=\frac{k_2}{k_1}\lvert t_E\rvert^2,\\
 R_H&=\lvert r_H\rvert^2,&
 T_H&=\frac{\varepsilon_1k_2}{\varepsilon_2k_1}\lvert t_H\rvert^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Substituting the Fresnel expressions gives \(R+T=1\) for this lossless,
propagating continuum problem. Transmission is not generally
\(\lvert t\rvert^2\). These weights apply to the stated component
normalizations; do not use the electric weight with magnetic-amplitude data.

Applying these /continuum/ weights to numerical amplitudes is a comparison
with continuum flux. It is not an exact discrete conservation identity.
Likewise, a conserved potential-energy diagnostic is a different observable
from normal Poynting flux. The measured quantity and the reference must remain
distinct in tables and plot labels.

* Homogeneous lattice dispersion

For spacings \(h_x,h_y,h_z\) and time step \(\Delta t\), define
\[
 K_a=\frac{2}{h_a}\sin\left(\frac{k_ah_a}{2}\right),\qquad
 W=\frac{2}{\Delta t}\sin\left(\frac{\omega\Delta t}{2}\right).
\]
Substitution of a Fourier mode into a centred second difference gives
\(-K_a^2\), and substitution into the leapfrog second time difference gives
\(-W^2\). Hence the homogeneous transverse mode obeys
\[
 \varepsilon_j W^2=K_x^2+K_y^2+K_z^2.
\]
This is [[ref-fallahi][Fallahi, Eq. (3.17)]] with the vacuum wave speed replaced
by \(1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}\). For the transverse, zero-gauge-residual
potential mode, the divergence correction vanishes and the same relation
applies; this deduction uses the staggered discrete calculus of
[[ref-ryu][Ryu et al., Appendix A, Eqs. (19)–(27)]]. It is not a claim about
an arbitrary potential state containing scalar or longitudinal modes.

For an aligned x-normal face, let \(K_t^2=K_y^2+K_z^2\). In the present
x-z experiments \(k_y=0\), but the oracle also accepts the general pair.
Solving the preceding relation for the positive normal root gives
\[
 q_j=\frac{h_x^2}{4}\left(\varepsilon_jW^2-K_t^2\right),\qquad
 k_j^{\mathrm{lat}}=\frac{2}{h_x}\arcsin\sqrt{q_j},
 \qquad 0<q_j<1.
\]
This is the propagating interior branch evaluated by =latticeNormal=.
The code also classifies complex evanescent and sawtooth branches. Endpoint
values \(q_j=0\) or \(q_j=1\) require separate treatment even when an API
classifies them with the real branch: they are not ordinary travelling modes
with nonzero normal group velocity.

For fixed transverse wavenumbers, differentiating the dispersion relation
gives the normal group velocity
\[
 v_{g,x}=\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k_x}
 =\frac{\sin(k_xh_x)}
 {h_x\varepsilon_jW\cos(\omega\Delta t/2)}.
\]
The differentiation is a deduction from [[ref-fallahi][Fallahi, Eq. (3.17)]],
using the group-velocity definition in [[ref-trefethen][Trefethen, §1,
Eqs. (1.4)–(1.5)]]. This velocity, evaluated over the retained pulse band,
is relevant to arrival times. A continuum speed or the central phase velocity
does not by itself bound the arrival of every spectral component.

The lattice wavevector, its group-velocity direction and the continuum angle
assigned to \((\omega,\kappa)\) need not coincide. This is a consequence of
the numerical dispersion relation, not a change of the requested continuum
reference angle [[ref-fallahi][Fallahi, §3.1.3]]. Record the actual frequencies,
wavenumbers, spacings and time step used for every comparison.

* Exact coefficients of the aligned discrete interface

The following derivation requires a flat material jump on a cell boundary,
uniform spacing normal to it, cell-centred material values, and the harmonic
normal-face permittivity used by the operator. It assumes the transverse field
sector discussed above. It does not apply unchanged to a staircase obtained
by sampling a tilted interface.

The recurrences follow by eliminating the complementary field from staggered
Faraday and Ampere differences: [[ref-yee][Yee, Eqs. (5)–(6)]], with the potential
field correspondence from [[ref-ryu][Ryu et al., Appendix A]]. The following
interface elimination is an explicit deduction, rather than a formula quoted
from either paper. It reproduces =latticeCoefficients= in
[[file:../../tests/apps/planar-interface/InterfaceOracles.h][InterfaceOracles.h]].
Here \(h=h_x\), and \(k_1,k_2\) denote /lattice/ normal roots throughout
this section.

** TE: two adjacent cell centres

The electric samples obey
\[
 E_{j+1}+E_{j-1}-2E_j
   +h^2\left(\varepsilon_jW^2-K_t^2\right)E_j=0.
\]
Place the interface at zero and the centres at \((j+\tfrac12)h\), with
medium 1 for \(j\le-1\) and medium 2 for \(j\ge0\). Away from the face,
the homogeneous recurrence has the two modes used above. At the adjacent
centres, each recurrence demands that the neighbouring value agree with the
extension of its own regional solution. Thus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e^{ik_1h/2}+r_E^{\mathrm{lat}}e^{-ik_1h/2}
   &=t_E^{\mathrm{lat}}e^{ik_2h/2},\\
 e^{-ik_1h/2}+r_E^{\mathrm{lat}}e^{ik_1h/2}
   &=t_E^{\mathrm{lat}}e^{-ik_2h/2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Eliminating the transmitted amplitude gives
\[
 2i\sin\left(\frac{(k_1-k_2)h}{2}\right)
   =2i r_E^{\mathrm{lat}}
      \sin\left(\frac{(k_1+k_2)h}{2}\right).
\]
Back-substitution then gives
\[
 r_E^{\mathrm{lat}}=
 \frac{\sin((k_1-k_2)h/2)}{\sin((k_1+k_2)h/2)},\qquad
 t_E^{\mathrm{lat}}=
 \frac{\sin(k_1h)}{\sin((k_1+k_2)h/2)}.
\]
No electric sample sits at the face in this reduced recurrence. Consequently,
the extrapolated amplitudes need not satisfy
\(t_E^{\mathrm{lat}}=1+r_E^{\mathrm{lat}}\) at finite spacing. In the
continuum limit, expanding each sine recovers the TE Fresnel expressions.
These statements follow from the displayed matching equations; they are not
extra interface conditions imposed on the numerical solution.

** TM: one stored interface-face value

Magnetic samples lie on normal faces \(u=mh\). The corresponding recurrence is
\[
 \frac{H_{m+1}-H_m}{\varepsilon_{m+1/2}}
 -\frac{H_m-H_{m-1}}{\varepsilon_{m-1/2}}
 +h^2\left(W^2-\frac{K_t^2}{\varepsilon_{x,m}}\right)H_m=0.
\]
In homogeneous regions \(\varepsilon_{x,m}\) is the regional permittivity.
At the material face the code uses
\[
 \varepsilon_{x,0}=
   \frac{2\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2}{\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2},
 \qquad
 \frac{1}{\varepsilon_{x,0}}
   =\frac12\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_2}\right).
\]
The harmonic construction for equal distances between a face and neighbouring
cell centres is derived in [[ref-eymard][Eymard et al., §7.1, Example 7.1]]. Its
use here is the actual constitutive rule in
[[file:../../core/material/O1VSCoefficients.h][O1VSCoefficients.h]],
not a general exact rule for an arbitrarily located or tilted material face.

There is one stored value at \(m=0\), so
\(H_0=1+r_H^{\mathrm{lat}}=t_H^{\mathrm{lat}}\). The adjacent values are
\[
 H_{-1}=e^{-ik_1h}+r_H^{\mathrm{lat}}e^{ik_1h},\qquad
 H_1=t_H^{\mathrm{lat}}e^{ik_2h}.
\]
The two homogeneous dispersion relations and the harmonic face value imply
\[
 h^2\left(W^2-\frac{K_t^2}{\varepsilon_{x,0}}\right)
 =\frac{1-\cos(k_1h)}{\varepsilon_1}
  +\frac{1-\cos(k_2h)}{\varepsilon_2}.
\]
Insert these expressions into the interface recurrence. Its cosine terms
cancel, leaving
\[
 \frac{\sin(k_1h)}{\varepsilon_1}(1-r_H^{\mathrm{lat}})
  =\frac{\sin(k_2h)}{\varepsilon_2}(1+r_H^{\mathrm{lat}}).
\]
Therefore
\[
 r_H^{\mathrm{lat}}=
 \frac{\varepsilon_2\sin(k_1h)-\varepsilon_1\sin(k_2h)}
      {\varepsilon_2\sin(k_1h)+\varepsilon_1\sin(k_2h)},\qquad
 t_H^{\mathrm{lat}}=1+r_H^{\mathrm{lat}}.
\]
This elimination completes the discrete deduction from the recurrence above.
Expanding the sines as the mesh is refined recovers the fixed-magnetic-basis
Fresnel expressions. A finite-grid Brewster minimum need not be at the
continuum Brewster angle: its numerator contains \(\sin(k_jh)\), rather than
\(k_j\). The two references answer different questions and should both be
identified explicitly.

* Initial pulse, field labels and measured coefficients

** A periodic pulse is a superposition of lattice modes

The aligned initializer superposes signed normal modes from the periodic box
with one fixed tangential wavenumber. Each normal mode is assigned its own
homogeneous lattice frequency. The Gaussian weights in normal wavenumber
localise the envelope normally to the face; a single tangential wavenumber
does not give transverse amplitude localisation. These properties follow from
the mode sum in [[file:../../apps/planar-interface/PlanePulse.h][PlanePulse.h]] and
the periodic phase condition in [[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.5,
Eq. (4.30)]]. The finite mode sum is periodic and is not a compactly supported
packet. Its tails and repeated copies must be considered in placement and timing.

For one scalar mode \(\psi=\cos(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x-\omega t)\),
the x-z TE start uses \(A_y=\psi\). With zero scalar potential, the time
difference gives the electric sample
\[
 E_y[k]=-W\sin\left(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x
                       -\omega(k-\tfrac12)\Delta t\right).
\]
The TM start uses the weighted discrete curl with signs
\(A_x=-D_z\psi\), \(A_z=+D_x\psi\) in the incident homogeneous medium.
Taking its curl gives \(B_y=\varepsilon_1W^2\psi\). Averaging the raw
magnetic values at consecutive integer times therefore gives
\[
 \overline B_y[k]=\varepsilon_1W^2\cos(\omega\Delta t/2)
 \cos\left(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x-\omega(k-\tfrac12)\Delta t\right).
\]
These are direct substitutions in the discrete curl/time reconstruction of
[[ref-ryu][Ryu et al., Appendix A]], specialised to the signs and labels in
[[file:../../core/base/LevelContract.h][LevelContract.h]] and =PlanePulse=.
The incident reference must include the same time average as the observed
magnetic channel. The factor \(\cos(\omega\Delta t/2)\) is not optional.

For the staggered field representation with R2 reconstruction, the relevant
labels are:

| Channel | Offset from its cell centre, in cell-width units | Time at completed step \(k\) |
|---------+--------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------|
| Electric y | \((0,1/2,0)\) | \((k-1/2)\Delta t\) |
| Averaged magnetic y | \((1/2,0,1/2)\) | \((k-1/2)\Delta t\) |
| Raw magnetic y | \((1/2,0,1/2)\) | \(k\Delta t\) |

This table is an implementation contract from =positionOffset= and
=halfStepLabel= in =LevelContract=; the general staggered construction is
described by [[ref-ryu][Ryu et al., Appendix A]]. In particular, electric and
magnetic components with the same integer probe index need not occupy the
same physical x-z point. Use the recorded physical component coordinates.

** Transform the same labelled signal in the measurement and reference

For a gate \(k_0\le k<k_0+N\), the running probe evaluates
\[
 \widehat F(\omega)=\Delta t\sum_{k=k_0}^{k_0+N-1}
 w_k F[k]e^{i\omega t_k}.
\]
Here \(t_k\) is the channel's actual label, and \(w_k\) is the selected
window. The definition is implemented by
[[file:../../opt-in/diagnostics/FourierProbe.h][FourierProbe.h]] and is the finite
sum corresponding to [[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.3, Eq. (4.6)]], with
the normalization stated above. There is no division by the number of samples
and no implicit spatial-area factor.

The analytic incident waveform must be evaluated at the same component
coordinates and labels and transformed with the same gate and window. A
finite real waveform has both frequency signs, so replacing its finite sum
by the amplitude of just one complex carrier is not generally equivalent.
Incident subtraction and normalization follow [[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson,
§4.3]]. A coefficient is usable only where the incident transform is sufficiently
resolved and nonzero; see also [[ref-schneider][Schneider, Eqs. (5.96)–(5.99)]].

** Transfer the probe phase to the interface

Let \(x_R\) be the incident-side probe, \(x_T\) the transmitted-side probe,
and \(x_I\) the requested interface position. Let \(k_i\) and \(k_t\) be
the /signed laboratory x components/ of the incident and transmitted waves;
the reflected component is \(k_r=-k_i\). This convention handles incidence
from either side. Let \(I\) denote the analytic incident transform at
\(x_R\), \(S\) the total transform there, and \(U\) the transmitted
transform at \(x_T\). For probes at the same tangential coordinate, write
each travelling amplitude as its face amplitude times its propagation factor.
Solving for the face ratios gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r(x_I)&=\left(\frac{S}{I}-1\right)
       e^{-2ik_i(x_I-x_R)},\\
 t(x_I)&=\frac{U}{I}
       e^{i[k_t(x_I-x_T)-k_i(x_I-x_R)]}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the two-probe, signed-wavevector version of the phase transfer derived
in [[ref-schneider][Schneider, §5.8.2, Eqs. (5.98)–(5.99)]]. The reflection
factor follows from the same calculation with \(k_r=-k_i\). In numerical
data, use the appropriate /lattice/ propagation roots for this propagation
correction, then compare the face ratios with the continuum reference at
the recorded \((\omega,\kappa)\). For differing tangential positions the
full spatial phase, including that tangential displacement, must be retained.

These expressions assume that the gates isolate the intended incident,
reflected and transmitted signals. A reference formula does not remove
contamination from the second material face, a periodic return or an outer
boundary. Qualify the chosen measurement by changing the relevant separation
or gate while still retaining the desired pulse. Use numerical group velocities
over the useful band when planning those checks
[[ref-trefethen][Trefethen, §§1–2]]; the final evidence comes from the selected
simulation controls, not from a central-frequency travel-time estimate alone.

** Reference-case labels

The labels below name reference problems in this demonstration. They are local
identifiers, not a numbering scheme from the literature and not a claim that a
case has been implemented or validated. A run must still state its geometry,
materials, illumination, observable and reference. Code, inputs, figures and
documentation that use an =RC-= label refer to these definitions.

| RC-2 | Oblique plane-pulse incidence from vacuum on a dielectric half-space, for TE and TM, including the Brewster condition. An angle scan may include the RC-1 normal-incidence limit. |

This document derives the plane-interface references needed for RC-1, RC-2 and
the continuum comparison and periodic measurement construction for RC-9.
Other entries fix the meaning of their labels; their
full reference derivations and measurement qualifications belong with their
implementations. The discrete potential invariant available in the solver must
be identified as such; it does not by itself establish the physical energy and

** RC-1: normal incidence and mesh refinement

Set \(\kappa=0\), choose the incident and transmitted permittivities, and
evaluate the normal-incidence continuum coefficients above. The reference is
frequency independent for these nondispersive media. The lattice reference
uses the actual \(\omega,\mathbf h,\Delta t\) and changes as the mesh or
time step changes [[ref-fallahi][Fallahi, Eq. (3.17)]].

A refinement comparison must keep the intended physical problem and coefficient
convention fixed. Record the actual wavelength resolution, the time-step policy,
the measured band and the isolation checks. Compare complex error and magnitude
error separately; phase is reported only for nonzero amplitudes. No observed
order follows merely from labelling three meshes as coarse, medium and fine.

** RC-2: oblique incidence and the angle scan

For a periodic tangential length \(L_t\), ordinary periodic fields require
\(\kappa L_t=2\pi m\), with integer \(m\). For a chosen central continuum
angle \(\theta_0>0\), phase matching therefore gives
\[
 \omega_0=\frac{\lvert\kappa\rvert}
 {\sqrt{\varepsilon_1}\sin\theta_0},\qquad
 \theta(\omega)=\arcsin\left(
 \frac{\lvert\kappa\rvert}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_1}\omega}\right).
\]
These are deductions from [[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.5, Eq. (4.30)
and pp. 85–86]]. A single fixed-\(\kappa\) pulse samples a range of angles
across its frequency band. Evaluate the Fresnel reference at each measured
frequency; do not use the central-angle coefficient for the entire band.

Keeping the cell shape fixed while selecting an integer transverse cell count
may require adjusting \(\omega_0\) to retain \(\theta_0\). In that case the
requested and actual cells per wavelength differ. Save the actual values and
evaluate the reference from them. The frequency axis, reference angle axis and
resolution labels must describe that same resolved row.

For the Brewster comparison, select the central continuum angle derived above
and compare the measured reflection with both the zero continuum value and the
generally nonzero finite-grid lattice value. This separates continuum agreement
from reproduction of the discrete interface. Absolute reflection error is the
appropriate quantity at the continuum zero [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §5.10]].

** RC-1 variant: shifted interface and phase origin

A requested continuum face and the transition represented by cell-centred
material sampling are distinct. For the current aligned sampling rule, a
small movement of the requested face can leave all cell values unchanged.
When the face passes a cell centre, a material value changes according to the
predicate's equality convention. The sampled transition then moves by a whole
cell. This follows directly from
[[file:../../core/material/PlanarSlab.h][PlanarSlab::contains]] and the
cell-centred material sampling; a shifted-face run must record both positions.

Let \(x_M\) denote the represented material face and \(x_I\) the requested
face. Re-expressing coefficients at a new phase origin gives
\[
 r(x_I)=r(x_M)e^{i(k_r-k_i)(x_I-x_M)},\qquad
 t(x_I)=t(x_M)e^{i(k_t-k_i)(x_I-x_M)}.
\]
This is the preceding phase-transfer derivation applied to two reference
positions [[ref-schneider][Schneider, §5.8.2]]. If the coefficients and roots
are propagating, this origin change affects phase without changing magnitude.
When \(x_I-x_M=O(h)\), expansion of the exponential gives an \(O(h)\)
complex discrepancy from registration alone for a nonzero coefficient. That
deduction does not imply an \(O(h)\) magnitude error, nor a universal observed
order when other errors are comparable.

The primary shifted-face comparison refers phase to the requested face.
Re-referring the same data to the represented face is useful for diagnosing
registration, but must be identified as a different comparison. It must not
silently replace the requested continuum geometry.

** RC-9: rotated interface and finite-width illumination

The continuum plane can be rotated about the x-z origin. For a normal rotated
from +x towards +z by \(\alpha\), its equation is
\[
 x\cos\alpha+z\sin\alpha=\delta.
\]
This is a direct rotation of \(x=\delta\); \(\delta\) remains the signed
normal distance from the origin. The continuum derivation above uses the
normal/tangent components and therefore applies with the chosen incidence
angle relative to this face [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §§5.6–5.8]]. This
geometric statement does not extend the aligned /discrete/ oracle to a
sampled tilted interface.

The choice of rotated initial modes, finite box, boundary conditions and
spectral extraction requires qualification. The incident direction and all
probe phases must agree with the chosen geometry. A fit to one outgoing
direction is not automatically the total outgoing power; spatial components
not represented by that fit must be considered
[[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.5, pp. 84–86]].

A finite transverse envelope is also possible. To see what it changes, let
\(v\) be the tangent coordinate and use the spatial transform with kernel
\(e^{-i\kappa v}\). A change of variable gives
\[
 \mathcal F_v\{a(v/w)e^{i\kappa_0v}\}(\kappa)
   =w\widehat a\bigl(w(\kappa-\kappa_0)\bigr),\qquad w>0.
\]
This is a Fourier scaling/shift deduction from the transform definition in
[[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, Eq. (4.6)]], applied to space with the stated
sign. Finite transverse width introduces a distribution of tangential
wavenumbers, hence a distribution of propagating incidence angles at each
frequency. Each continuum plane-wave component has its own Fresnel coefficient.
Assigning the whole beam only the central-angle coefficient is an approximation;
it needs a beam-width study or an angular-spectrum reference. In particular,
the noncentral components of a beam centred at Brewster need not have zero
reflection [[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §5.10]].

Comparable aligned and rotated runs must match the media, incident side,
polarization, interface-relative incidence, physical spectral content and
observable conventions. If finite illumination is used, include an aligned
control with the same beam definition and extraction. Document the resolution
and time-step policy and any unavoidable differences in the resolved rows.
Assess boundary, angular/reference and extraction effects before attributing a
difference to the interface discretization. The following section specifies one
periodic construction. Its derivation does not establish measured convergence.

* Exactly periodic tilted-plane construction
<<tilted-construction>>

This section describes the =periodic_tilted_plane= path for vacuum incidence
onto a dielectric with \(\varepsilon_d\geq1\), using Standard differences,
O1-VS and R2. The interface tilt \(\alpha\) and incidence angle \(\theta\)
are independent signed inputs, with \(0<\lvert\alpha\rvert<90^\circ\) and
\(\lvert\theta\rvert<90^\circ\). The construction changes the compatible
cell shape when needed; it does not round the requested tilt to a grid diagonal.
It uses a normal-localised periodic pulse, without a finite tangent envelope.
The aligned discrete-interface coefficients are not an oracle for its staircase.

** Normal coordinates, signed face offset and material samples

Write
\[
 c=\cos\alpha>0,\quad s=\sin\alpha,\quad \beta=\lvert s\rvert,
 \quad\eta=\operatorname{sign}s,\qquad
 \mathbf n=(c,0,s),\quad\mathbf t=(-s,0,c).
\]
Here \(c,s\) are trigonometric factors; the vacuum wave speed remains one.
The requested face is \(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf x=\delta\). In this
section the recorded coordinate \(u\) increases towards the incident vacuum:
an incoming wave travels towards decreasing \(u\). This orientation differs
from the incident-to-transmitted coordinate used in the introductory Fresnel
derivation. The fixed \(E_y\) and \(H_y\) amplitude bases remain unchanged.
The formulas below are coordinate/reciprocal-lattice deductions from the
plane-wave phase and Bloch relation in [[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.5, Eq. (4.30)]].

For positive coprime integers \(p,q\) and a positive integer \(M\), choose
\[
 h_z=\frac{cp}{\beta q}h_x,\quad N_x=Mp,\quad N_z=Mq,\quad
 L_x=N_xh_x,\quad L_z=N_zh_z,\quad P=cL_x=\beta L_z.
\]
The primitive material translation and its tangent length are
\[
 \mathbf T_0=(-\eta ph_x,0,qh_z),\qquad
 \mathbf n\cdot\mathbf T_0=0,\qquad
 L_0=\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf T_0=ph_x/\beta,\qquad L_t=ML_0.
\]
The primitive normal row spacing is \(d_n=ch_x/q\). At cell centres,
\[
 \mathbf n\cdot\mathbf x_{ik}
 =\frac{d_n}{2}\,[q(2i+1)+\eta p(2k+1)].
\]
Thus the bracket is an integer row label; Cartesian box translations change
it by integer multiples of \(2Mpq\). =TiltedMaterialMap= wraps that integer
before applying the signed offset. With dielectric width \(D\), incident
width \(H\), and \(D+H=P\), the sampled dielectric satisfies
\[
 \zeta=(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf x_{ik}-\delta)\bmod P,
 \qquad \zeta=0\ \text{or}\ H\leq\zeta<P.
\]
This is the closed interval \([\delta-D,\delta]\), repeated normally.
At zero offset, integer comparisons resolve exact interface ties. Nonzero
offsets use the recorded floating-point offset after integer wrapping. This is
centre sampling with no sub-cell average. A two-dimensional staircase has no
single represented-face displacement comparable to the aligned scalar \(x_M\).

The phase anchor chosen by =tiltedAnchor= is
\[
 \mathbf a=(-\eta L_x/2,0,L_z/2)+\delta\mathbf n,
 \qquad\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a=\delta,
 \qquad\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf a=L_t/2.
\]
For a lifted point \(\widetilde{\mathbf x}\), the stored coordinates are
\(u=\mathbf n\cdot(\widetilde{\mathbf x}-\mathbf a)\) and
\(v=\mathbf t\cdot(\widetilde{\mathbf x}-\mathbf a)\). Moving
\(\delta\) therefore moves the face and the source/probe construction
consistently along the normal, while retaining the tangent origin.

** Exactly periodic carriers and the incidence convention

Let \(a,j\) be integers, and set \(b=j+\eta a\). The Cartesian carrier
\[
 k_x=2\pi a/L_x,\qquad k_z=2\pi b/L_z
\]
has unit phase under both Cartesian box translations. These identities are
exact algebraically; their numerical evaluations retain floating-point roundoff.
Taking its normal and
tangent projections gives
\[
 \kappa=-s k_x+c k_z=\frac{2\pi j}{L_t},\qquad
 k_n=c k_x+s k_z=\frac{2\pi}{P}(a+\eta s^2j).
\]
Varying \(a\) at fixed \(j\) constructs a packet in the normal coordinate
with one tangent wavenumber. A pure normal translation by \(P\) gives the
common phase \(\exp(2\pi i\eta s^2j)\), which need not equal one.
Periodicity is imposed in Cartesian coordinates; it must not be replaced by an
assumed independent unit-periodic normal coordinate. These identities follow
by substitution in the Bloch phase, not by imposing an additional boundary rule.

The recorded incidence angle is the signed /continuum reference angle/
\[
 \theta(\omega)=\arcsin\!\frac{\kappa}{\omega}
\]
for vacuum incidence. Consequently, for nonzero requested \(\theta_0\),
the resolved frequency is \(\omega_0=\kappa/\sin\theta_0\); at normal
incidence \(j=0\) and the requested frequency is retained. This is the
fixed-tangent-wavenumber frequency/angle relation of
[[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.5, pp. 85–86]] and
[[ref-ellingson][Ellingson, §§5.6–5.8]], with a signed tangent convention.
The lattice phase angle \(\operatorname{atan2}(\kappa,-k_n^-)\) need
not equal \(\theta\); a group-velocity direction is a third quantity.
The C++ continuum utility returns the angle's nonnegative magnitude. Retain
the sign of =kappa= and =actual_angle_degrees= when plotting signed incidence.

The automatic layout search quantises \(j\), adjusts \(\omega_0\), and
increases \(M\) until the estimated isolation widths fit. The requested
and actual resolution and cell shape are separate metadata. Automatic primitive
candidates use coprime \(p,q\leq6\), ranked by the logarithmic change in
the requested z/x cell ratio; an explicit pair may use values up to 16.
These are current search limits, not restrictions of the carrier algebra. An explicitly
pinned =period_cells_multiplier= and signed =tangent_mode= require an explicit
primitive pair. =resolvePinnedTiltedPeriod= checks their angle/sign consistency
and rejects a period too short for the recomputed gates and probe layout.
Scaling \(M,j\) by the same positive integer keeps \(\kappa\), and hence
\(\omega_0\) at fixed nonzero incidence, unchanged. The remaining normal
period is assigned to the incident layer; =separation_factor= separately
enlarges the minimum layer widths. Neither mechanism silently changes the tilt.

** Four directional roots and a sufficient principal-branch restriction

For a candidate normal wavenumber, define
\[
 k_x=ck_n-s\kappa,\qquad k_z=sk_n+c\kappa,\qquad
 F(k_n)=\frac{4\sin^2(k_xh_x/2)}{h_x^2}
       +\frac{4\sin^2(k_zh_z/2)}{h_z^2},\qquad
 W(\omega)=\frac{2\sin(\omega\Delta t/2)}{\Delta t}.
\]
In medium \(\varepsilon\), the roots solve
\(F(k_n)=\varepsilon W^2\). This is the rotated-coordinate substitution
in [[ref-fallahi][Fallahi, Eq. (3.17)]], with wave speed
\(1/\sqrt\varepsilon\). Differentiating at fixed \(\kappa\), following
[[ref-trefethen][Trefethen, §1, Eqs. (1.4)–(1.5)]], gives
\[
 v_n=\left.\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k_n}\right|_\kappa
 =\frac{c\sin(k_xh_x)/h_x+s\sin(k_zh_z)/h_z}
        {\varepsilon W\cos(\omega\Delta t/2)}.
\]
For \(0<\omega\Delta t<\pi\), the denominator is positive. The
two roots are labelled by the sign of \(v_n\), independently in each
medium. Use \(k_i^-\), \(k_i^+\), \(k_d^-\), \(k_d^+\) for
the four directions. The reflected root is \(k_i^+\), not an assumed
\(-k_i^-\): at fixed nonzero \(\kappa\), \(F(k_n)\) need not be even.

The implemented root search deliberately restricts the admissible branch.
Within \(\lvert k_xh_x\rvert,\lvert k_zh_z\rvert<\pi/2\),
\[
 F''(k_n)=2c^2\cos(k_xh_x)+2s^2\cos(k_zh_z)>0.
\]
Hence there is at most one grazing minimum \(k_g\). The code requires
the derivative to bracket that minimum and the target to lie above it, then
bisects each monotone side. In addition it requires
\[
 K\max(h_x,h_z)<\sqrt2,\qquad K=\sqrt\varepsilon\,W.
\]
For any Cartesian principal root, \(\lvert K_x\rvert,\lvert K_z\rvert
\leq K\), with \(K_\ell=2\sin(k_\ell h_\ell/2)/h_\ell\).
The displayed bound therefore implies both strict phase inequalities above.
Thus the two roots exhaust this qualified principal branch. This is a
sufficient-bound deduction from Fallahi's symbols, not a general solver for
all dispersion branches or a stability/accuracy theorem. The zero-frequency,
zero-\(\kappa\) timing endpoint uses the limiting speeds
\(v_n=\pm1/\sqrt\varepsilon\); measured coefficient bins remain positive
frequency. Unsupported branches are rejected before allocating the simulation.

** Material-period Floquet orders

The material period along the tangent is \(L_0\), not the larger box
period \(L_t\). Its possible scattering orders have
\[
 \kappa_\ell=\kappa+2\pi\ell/L_0,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb Z,
\]
by the reciprocal-period argument in [[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.5, p. 84]].
The following is a sufficient /discrete/ exclusion bound, derived here.
For each Cartesian direction \(m=x,z\), let
\[
 x_m=\min(1,h_mK/2),\qquad C_m=\frac{\arcsin x_m}{x_m},\qquad C_m(0)=1.
\]
On the principal branch, monotonicity of \(\arcsin x/x\) gives
\(\lvert k_m\rvert\leq C_m\lvert K_m\rvert\). Applying
Cauchy–Schwarz to the tangent projection then gives
\[
 \lvert -s k_x+c k_z\rvert\leq
 B_t(\varepsilon,\omega)
 :=K\sqrt{s^2C_x^2+c^2C_z^2}.
\]
Since every nonzero order has
\(\lvert\kappa_\ell\rvert\geq2\pi/L_0-\lvert\kappa\rvert\),
\[
 m_F(\omega)=2\pi/L_0-\lvert\kappa\rvert
             -\max_{\varepsilon\in\{1,\varepsilon_d\}}B_t(\varepsilon,\omega)>0
\]
excludes all nonzero propagating orders in either homogeneous medium.
For the supported \(\varepsilon_d\geq1\), the dielectric gives the larger
bound and is the one evaluated by =tiltedOrderMargin=.

The record's =floquet_margin= covers the timing samples and requested
measurement bins; =retained_floquet_margin= checks every retained carrier.
These two finite sets must remain distinguishable. A positive margin does not
exclude evanescent orders, prove a negligible field near the staircase, or
qualify the finite observation gate. Those require separate extraction and
distance/layout controls.

** Retained packet, weighted curl and omitted-mode accounting

Let \(k_0=k_i^-(\omega_0)\), \(f\) be =cutoff_fraction=, and
\(\tau\) be =tail_sigmas=. The implementation sets
\[
 \sigma_k=\frac{(1-f)(k_g-k_0)}{\tau},\qquad
 g_a=\exp\!\left[-\frac{(k_{n,a}-k_0)^2}{2\sigma_k^2}\right].
\]
The finite Cartesian principal carrier set satisfies
\(2\lvert a\rvert<N_x\) and \(2\lvert j+\eta a\rvert<N_z\).
Within it, the retained carriers have
\(\lvert k_{n,a}-k_0\rvert<\tau\sigma_k\) and negative incident
group velocity. Each retained carrier must satisfy the continuum propagation,
directional-root and Floquet checks. The source modes and weights are written
to =modes.csv=; requested observation bins need not coincide with them.
The current setup requires at least 16 retained modes, \(\tau>3\),
\(0\leq f<1\), and strictly positive \(f\) for nonzero incidence.
These guards do not themselves establish spectral or extraction accuracy.
The observation grid is uniform in normal wavenumber around \(k_0\), then
mapped through the incident dispersion; it is not generally uniform in frequency.

For source centre \(\mathbf a+S\mathbf n\), define
\[
 \psi(\mathbf x,t)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal R}g_a
 \cos\!\left[k_{n,a}(u-S)+\kappa v-\omega_a t\right].
\]
The scalar potential is initialised to zero. TE uses \(A_y=\psi\).
TM uses the compatible staggered differences
\[
 A_x=-D_z\psi/\varepsilon_x,\qquad
 A_z=D_x\psi/\varepsilon_z.
\]
Here the face coefficients are the harmonic coefficients used by the solver.
With the same coefficients in the weighted divergence,
\(D_x(\varepsilon_xA_x)+D_z(\varepsilon_zA_z)
=-D_xD_z\psi+D_zD_x\psi=0\).
This cancellation is a discrete-curl deduction from
[[ref-ryu][Ryu et al., Appendix A, Eqs. (19)–(24)]], with the weighted gauge
quantity identified in [[ref-chew][Chew, Eqs. (14), (15), (19)]]. It does not
establish that a nonzero packet tail already overlapping the material is a
pure incoming homogeneous solution. That error still needs qualification.

For vacuum incident modes, the reconstructed channel weights are
\[
 Q_a^{\rm TE}=W_a,\qquad
 Q_a^{\rm TM}=W_a^2\cos(\omega_a\Delta t/2),\qquad
 W_a=W(\omega_a).
\]
They follow from the same centred time difference and R2 magnetic average
derived above; the time labels are those of =LevelContract=. Both are
nonnegative on the supported positive-frequency branch. For the omitted
members \(\mathcal O\) of the enumerated principal carrier set, the
triangle inequality gives the homogeneous channel-amplitude bound
\[
 \lvert F_{\mathcal O}(\mathbf x,t)\rvert
 \leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal O}g_aQ_a,
 \qquad
 f_{\rm omit}=\frac{\sum_{a\in\mathcal O}g_aQ_a}
                    {\sum_{a\in\mathcal R}g_aQ_a}.
\]
=discarded_field_weight_fraction= is this L1 weight ratio. Its denominator is
the retained /weight sum/, not a lower bound on the actual field at an arbitrary
point. It is not an energy fraction, a relative error in a weak coefficient,
a bound on an infinite continuum Gaussian tail, or a bound on interface and
return contamination. This is a triangle-inequality deduction for the stated
finite superposition. Increasing the box changes modal density and therefore
the unnormalised pulse amplitude because each carrier retains its Gaussian
weight. Compare incident-normalised coefficients, and retain the actual spectrum
when assessing whether two linear scattering experiments match.

** Four-direction envelope timing and return paths

Write the positive speed magnitudes as
\(v_i^-=-v_n(k_i^-)\), \(v_i^+=v_n(k_i^+)\), and similarly
\(v_d^-,v_d^+\). For ideal point probes at \(u=\pm d\), source
distance \(S\), dielectric width \(D\), incident width \(H\), and
\(P=D+H\), the centre travel times are:

| Path | Centre travel time |
|------+--------------------|
| Desired reflection | \(S/v_i^-+d/v_i^+\) |
| Desired transmission | \(S/v_i^-+d/v_d^-\) |
| Second-face return to transmitted probe | \(S/v_i^-+D/v_d^-+(D-d)/v_d^+\) |
| Second-face return to incident probe | \(S/v_i^-+D/v_d^-+D/v_d^++d/v_i^+\) |
| Negative wrap to incident probe | \((S+H-d)/v_i^-+D/v_d^-\) |
| Positive reflected wrap to transmitted probe | \(S/v_i^-+H/v_i^++(D-d)/v_d^+\) |
| Analytic homogeneous incident image | \((S-d+P)/v_i^-\) |
| Complete negative wrap to transmitted probe | \((S+H)/v_i^-+(D+d)/v_d^-\) |
| Complete positive wrap to incident probe | \(S/v_i^-+(H+d)/v_i^++D/v_d^+\) |

Each entry is the sum of its directional path lengths divided by the
corresponding group speeds. It is a ray-envelope deduction from the
group-velocity interpretation in [[ref-trefethen][Trefethen, §§1–2]], under the
one-propagating-order hypothesis checked above. Reflected and reverse-medium
speeds must not be replaced by their opposite-direction counterparts.

The nominal initial half-width is \(w=\tau/\sigma_k\), rounded up to
primitive normal rows. At each sampled frequency the timing interval is
the displayed centre time plus or minus \(w/v_i^-\). =TiltedTiming::window=
takes the earliest and latest values across its finite normal-wavenumber sample.
Finite probe boxes use a conservative interval \([d_{\min},d_{\max}]\)
from their component-coordinate extent: desired gates use the latest arrival,
and return exclusions use the earliest. The code rounds gates up to integer
steps, adds three guard steps and =gate_padding_steps=, then chooses layer
widths and a compatible period. These guards and sampled extrema are setup
choices, not an analytic error bound. A truncated Gaussian mode sum has no
compact normal support; dispersive broadening, tails and finite sampling
remain reasons to compare longer gates and larger separations/periods.

** Finite-gate analytic incident transform

The positive-kernel DTFT follows [[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.3, Eq. (4.6)]],
with the common \(1/(2\pi)\) omitted as above. For the half-step labels
\(t_m=(m-1/2)\Delta t\), \(m=0,\ldots,G-1\), a finite geometric sum gives
\[
 \mathcal D_G(\nu)=\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{G-1}e^{i\nu t_m}
 =\Delta t\,G\,\frac{\operatorname{sinc}(Ga)}{\operatorname{sinc}(a)}
       e^{ia(G-2)},\qquad a=\nu\Delta t/2,
 \quad\operatorname{sinc}(a)=\frac{\sin a}{a}.
\]
This is a direct summation deduction, not a continuous/infinite-time transform.
=tiltedSinc= uses its limiting expansion at zero. The admitted positive
frequencies keep \(\omega\pm\omega_a\) away from nonzero exact temporal
aliases where the written quotient would also need its removable limit.

Set \(\phi_a=k_{n,a}(u-S)+\kappa v\), and
\(C_a^\pm=e^{\pm i\phi_a}\mathcal D_G(\omega\mp\omega_a)\).
The analytic incident channels are
\[
 \widehat E_y^{\rm inc}=\sum_a g_a\frac{iW_a}{2}(C_a^+-C_a^-),\qquad
 \widehat H_y^{\rm inc}=\sum_a g_a
 \frac{W_a^2\cos(\omega_a\Delta t/2)}{2}(C_a^++C_a^-).
\]
These expressions expand the real sine/cosine channels, so both temporal
frequency signs remain in the finite gate. The incident normalization uses
the incident probe's actual gate; the transmitted gate may differ and is
recorded separately. The analytic sum is exact for the retained homogeneous
incident mode history, up to floating-point evaluation. It does not make a
finite-gate fit an exact monochromatic scattering measurement.

** Lifted point spectra and complex amplitude projection

For each wrapped component point \((x,y,z)\), store integer windings
\(\ell_x,\ell_z\) and use
\[
 \widetilde{\mathbf x}=(x+\ell_xL_x,y,z+\ell_zL_z).
\]
The implementation chooses a common lift from the anchor's box windings and
rejects a probe patch crossing a Cartesian edge or intersecting the sampled
interface, including the surrounding component stencils. It does not silently
snap or split that patch. Both windings can be nonzero for signed offsets.
An initial carrier is unchanged by these Cartesian translations because its
phase increments are integer multiples of \(2\pi\). An outgoing root fitted
on a local homogeneous patch need not itself be a global Cartesian box mode;
the same lift is therefore essential to its face phase. This follows directly
from the carrier phase identities and [[ref-schneider][Schneider's propagation correction]].

At frequency \(\omega\), form one unit-modulus basis per direction,
\(b_j(k_n)=\exp[i(k_nu_j+\kappa v_j)]\). For point data \(f_j\),
expanding the least-squares objective gives
\[
 \sum_j\lvert f_j-A b_j\rvert^2
 =\sum_j\lvert f_j\rvert^2
  -2\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{\overline A\sum_jf_j\overline b_j\right\}
  +N_p\lvert A\rvert^2,
 \qquad
 A=\frac1{N_p}\sum_j f_j\overline b_j.
\]
The last equality is the minimiser of the displayed quadratic; this is the
unweighted one-column projection used by =fitTiltedAmplitude=. Its physical
incident-subtraction/phase convention follows
[[ref-oskooi][Oskooi and Johnson, §4.3]] and [[ref-schneider][Schneider, §5.8.2]];
the minimisation algebra is given here explicitly.

Let \(I_j\) be the analytic incident transform, \(U_j\) the incident-side
total transform, and \(V_j\) the transmitted-side transform. Fit
\[
 A_i=\langle I_j\overline{b_j(k_i^-)}\rangle_i,\quad
 A_r=\langle(U_j-I_j)\overline{b_j(k_i^+)}\rangle_i,\quad
 A_t=\langle V_j\overline{b_j(k_d^-)}\rangle_t,\qquad
 r=A_r/A_i,\quad t=A_t/A_i.
\]
The fitted amplitudes already refer to the anchor; no additional aligned
point-probe phase factor is applied. For each fit the recorded residual is
\[
 \rho=\frac{\sqrt{\langle\lvert f_j-A b_j\rvert^2\rangle}}
                {\lvert A_i\rvert}.
\]
The denominator is the incident amplitude even for the reflected fit; this
avoids dividing by a vanishing physical reflection. All three residuals must
be at most =fit_residual_limit=, and \(\lvert A_i\rvert\) must be at least
=incident_floor= times the largest fitted incident amplitude among selected
bins. Failed bins retain their raw spectra and fitted amplitudes; ratios are
invalid and carry a reason.

A residual measures the part of the data outside the fitted basis. A parallel
contaminating component can bias \(A\) while leaving a small residual.
Positive Floquet margins do not remove evanescent orders, and finite gates can
mix neighbouring source modes. Therefore retain incoming and scattered residuals,
change probe distance/extent, compare homogeneous starts, and vary gates/layout
before attributing a continuum discrepancy to material discretisation. These
are qualifications, not a claimed universal accuracy threshold. Continuum
power weights applied to fitted amplitudes remain continuum flux estimates,
not an exact discrete energy balance or an integration of unmeasured orders.

** Tilted output contract and comparison scope

Plane schema two sets =geometry= to =periodic_tilted_plane=. The record
retains the normal/tangent vectors, signed requested face and anchor, primitive
and box periods, actual spacing and requested shape, signed tangent mode and
wavenumber, actual frequency vector, source placement, gates, omitted-weight
ratio, Floquet margins and fit criteria. =spectra.csv= stores all three signed
normal roots and velocities, the complex incident/reflected/transmitted face
amplitudes, dimensionless \(r,t\), residuals, validity and reasons.
=probe-spectra.csv= stores the original point ordering, actual wrapped
coordinates, both windings, \(u,v\), and raw analytic-incident/total transforms.
Transmitted-side incident columns are zero placeholders, not a second incident
normalization. Raw transforms and face amplitudes remain in normalized solver
units even when the separate displayed angular-frequency column uses SI units.

The Python raw-data audit recomputes the fits and ratios using the recorded
directional roots; agreement checks extraction consistency, not the correctness
of those roots or the scattering model. The independent C++ continuum reference
uses the recorded \(\omega,\kappa\) and media. A tilted run must request
continuum-only reference output; it must never inherit aligned lattice
coefficients merely because those functions are available. Matched controls
must preserve the actual media, frequency, \(\kappa\), cell/time-step policy
and intended pulse band, while recording deliberate layout or extraction changes.

The existing =spectra.csv= columns =r_real=, =r_imag=, =t_real= and =t_imag=
are the parts of the face-referred measured coefficients, not reference values.
The frequency column is =angular_frequency= in solver units or
=angular_frequency_rad_per_s= for SI output. Reference calculations must use
the corresponding converted units and recorded polarization. Raw point/box
spectra and derived coefficients have different meanings; retain their labels
when additional output columns are introduced.

* Reference data and reproducibility
<<provenance>>

For every reported comparison, preserve the actual reference table alongside
the measured data. A formula and a plot alone do not identify which numerical
reference was used. The record should identify:

- The input file and resolved physical/numerical row: media, incident side,
  polarization, geometry, frequency vector, wavevector, mesh and time step.
- The measured channel, physical probe coordinates, time labels, gate/window,
  transform normalization and interface phase origin.
- The reference type, applicable assumptions, source revision, exact evaluation
  command/inputs, numerical output and the version of this derivation.
- The conversion from externally supplied reference data, if any, including
  source/version, units, basis and every extraction or interpolation step.
- The measured files and comparison/figure commands, valid-bin criteria and
  omitted bins, together with boundary, angular and extraction control runs.

Keep continuum references, discrete references and measured spectra separately
identifiable. Store the selected raw data required to reconstruct derived
coefficients and figures. The reference utility reuses the host formula
functions listed above; its actual interface and Python invocation records are
specified in [[file:README.md][the reference-tools README]]. Preserve =modes.csv= and
=probe-spectra.csv= for tilted data alongside the run record and derived spectra.
No output should imply that a reference calculation has been verified merely
because the utility ran successfully.
